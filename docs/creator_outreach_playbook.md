# Creator Outreach Playbook

Internal helper-agent file. Outreach is manual, low-volume, and logged in `data/creators.csv`.

## Target Categories

- Gaming nostalgia pages.
- DS, PS2, Xbox 360, GameCube, handheld, and cheat-device archive pages.
- Old internet and catalog-scan pages.
- Experimental graphic design pages.
- Streetwear micro-pages.
- Poster, sticker, and physical media collectors.
- Anime/gaming streetwear creators.
- Resale and archive fashion pages.
- Newsletter writers covering internet culture, gaming memory, or independent brands.

## Contact Rules

- No mass DMs.
- No scraping.
- No repeated follow-ups.
- No pretending the artifact is real history.
- No asking them to hide that a placement is paid.
- Send one specific post or proof, not a pitch deck.
- Log every contact attempt.

## Repost Pitch

```text
found your archive posts through the handheld scans.
we make fictional recovered Action Replay files and one of them fits your page.
no pressure. if you repost it, please leave the weird filename/crop visible.
```

## Product Proof Pitch

```text
we archived the first run like a broken import listing instead of cleaning it up.
thought you might like the tag / sticker / inventory side more than the normal product shot.
```

## Paid Placement Question

```text
do you take paid reposts for archive/product posts?
if yes, send rate, format, and whether story/feed is available.
we will not ask you to claim it is real history.
```

## What To Track

In `data/creators.csv`, track:

- handle,
- platform,
- target category,
- audience estimate,
- why they fit,
- contact status,
- quoted rate,
- permission status,
- placement result,
- saves/shares/comments if reported,
- next follow-up date.

## Good Response Signal

- They notice a specific artifact detail.
- They ask for the file crop, sticker, tag, or product proof.
- Their audience comments with recognition, not just "fire."
- The placement creates profile visits or saves.

## Bad Response Signal

- They only sell follower packages.
- They promise guaranteed engagement.
- They ask for fake claims.
- Their audience looks unrelated.
- Their page is mostly recycled engagement bait.
